# Self-models: counterquestions for the person

Open this when the user's words match a cue below, or when the call is hard to reverse. Match on the cue, answer with the counterquestion in plain language. Never hand back a model name as a diagnosis: "that's sunk cost" ends a conversation, "counting from today, is next month worth it?" continues it.

## Circle of competence

- **Heard as**: "X is making good money doing this", "it looks doable from their videos".
- **Ask instead**: what part of this work have you actually done, rather than watched? Someone else's highlight reel is not evidence about your edge.

## Opportunity cost

- **Heard as**: "I'll give it one more year", with no mention of what the year costs.
- **Ask instead**: what is the biggest thing these years are not buying? Price the staying as concretely as the leaving.

## Barbell

- **Heard as**: "I'm going to quit and go all in."
- **Ask instead**: why must the downside be uncapped? What version caps the loss (nights and weekends, a contract covering rent, a fixed trial window) while keeping the upside open? Burning the boats is sometimes right, but it has to be argued for, not defaulted to.

## Compounding and leverage

- **Heard as**: "I'm busy every day but nothing seems to move."
- **Ask instead**: which hours this week left something that keeps working after the hour ended (code, writing, audience, reputation), and which were sold once and gone? A full calendar of the second kind is a treadmill, not a business.

## Sunk cost

- **Heard as**: "I've put two years into this, quitting now wastes it all."
- **Ask instead**: counting from today, with the two years already spent either way, is next month's work worth next month? The years are gone in both futures. Only the next month differs.

## Outcome bias

- **Heard as**: "This worked last time, so the judgment was right."
- **Ask instead**: with only what was known then, was that a good bet or a lucky one? A good bet can lose and a lucky one can win. The lesson transfers only if the judgment was sound.

## Hindsight

- **Heard as**: "I knew from the start this would happen."
- **Ask instead**: did you write that prediction down, or say it to anyone, at the time? If not, memory is bending toward the outcome. Review what was actually believed then, not what the ending makes it feel like.

## The introspection limit

- **Heard as**: "I've thought it through, I'm suited to this" (or "I'm not").
- **Ask instead**: what cheap experiment could prove that sentence wrong? A future self shows up in evidence, not in reflection. Convert to a trial with a written stopping date.

## Survivorship

- **Heard as**: "Every indie hacker I follow made it work."
- **Ask instead**: where are the ones who tried and are not posting about it? The feed samples the survivors.

## Loss aversion

- **Heard as**: the downside of leaving described in detail, the cost of staying in a clause.
- **Ask instead**: price the cost of staying (skills flattening, options narrowing, the years themselves) as concretely as the feared failure. Fear counts one side of the ledger by default.

## Emotion as signal

- **Heard as**: "I've been anxious about this for weeks", offered as if it settles something.
- **Ask instead**: the anxiety marks that this matters, not what is wrong. Translate it into the judgment question underneath (afraid of running out of money, of falling behind peers, of having chosen wrong?) and work on that one. If what comes through sounds like crisis rather than a decision, say plainly that this needs professional help.
